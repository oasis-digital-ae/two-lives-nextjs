Why High Performers Don't Trust Themselves Anymore

2026-06-25 • By Basim Yafai

Why High Performers Don't Trust Themselves Anymore

 

Somewhere Along the Way, Your Own Voice Became Quieter

When I first started working with high performers, I assumed confidence was the biggest challenge they faced.

I was wrong.

Whether I was speaking with an entrepreneur growing a business, an athlete preparing for competition, a leader carrying responsibility, or a poker player making decisions under pressure, I kept noticing the same pattern. They weren't struggling because they lacked intelligence or experience. Most of them had spent years proving they were capable.

The real struggle was far quieter than that.

Somewhere along the way, they had stopped trusting themselves.

Not all at once, but gradually. Decision after decision. Expectation after expectation. Until their own voice became harder to hear than everybody else's.

The people who looked the most certain on the outside were often questioning themselves the most on the inside.

 

Success Can Pull You Away From Yourself

Success brings opportunities, but it also brings noise.

As your responsibilities grow, so do the opinions around you. Clients have expectations. Investors want answers. Supporters offer advice. Social media tells you what success should look like, while comparison quietly convinces you someone else is doing it better.

Without noticing, you begin making decisions through a different filter.

Instead of asking, "What feels right to me?", the questions become:

  • What will people expect?
  • What's the safest decision?
  • What if I get this wrong?
  • What if this changes how people see me?

Over time, your intuition becomes drowned out by pressure.

Not because you've lost it.

Because you've stopped listening to it.

 

I See This More Often Than People Realise

One of the biggest misconceptions about high performers is that they always know what they're doing.

The reality is often very different.

Some of the most successful people I've worked with have reached points where they questioned almost every decision they made. Not because they suddenly became less capable, but because success often increases the weight of every choice.

An entrepreneur starts overthinking opportunities they would once have embraced.

An athlete begins second-guessing instincts that were once automatic.

A leader hesitates because every decision affects other people.

A poker player replays hands long after leaving the table, wondering whether to trust experience or fear.

Different environments... The same internal conversation.

 

The Question Beneath the Question

I've come to believe that most people aren't really asking,

"How do I become more confident?"

They're asking something much deeper.

"Can I trust myself again?"

There is an important difference.

Confidence often comes and goes depending on circumstances.

Self-trust is different.

It allows you to make decisions even when certainty doesn't exist. It gives you the courage to move forward without needing every answer first, because you know you'll be able to handle whatever happens next.

Confidence is about believing you'll succeed. Self-trust is believing you'll be okay even if you don't.

 

Rediscovering Your Own Direction

This is one of the ideas that sits at the heart of Two Lives Theory.

As high performers, we spend years learning from mentors, coaches, books, experiences and the people around us. All of that is valuable.

But there comes a point where growth asks something different.

Instead of collecting more answers, it asks whether you've stayed connected to your own.

I've seen people build incredible businesses while ignoring what they genuinely wanted. I've seen athletes continue chasing goals that no longer inspired them because they felt they had to. I've seen leaders continue carrying responsibilities they had quietly outgrown because stepping away felt unfamiliar.

The transition isn't about becoming someone new.

It's about reconnecting with the person you've been unable to hear beneath the noise.

Sometimes the next chapter doesn't begin with another achievement.

Sometimes it begins with finally trusting yourself enough to choose a different direction.

 

Something Worth Reflecting On...

If you stripped away the expectations, opinions and pressure for just a moment...

What decision would you make?

Would you still be chasing the same goal?

Would you still be building the same business?

Would you still be saying yes to the same opportunities?

... Or would you choose differently?

Sometimes the answers to those questions tell us far more than another motivational quote ever could.

 

My Final Thoughts

Trusting yourself isn't about having all the answers.

It's about believing that your worth isn't determined by making perfect decisions.

The entrepreneurs, athletes, leaders and elite competitors I admire most aren't the ones who never doubt themselves.

They're the ones who have learned to hear their own voice again, even when the world around them is loud.

Perhaps that's the next stage of growth, not becoming someone more successful...But becoming someone more connected to themselves.
