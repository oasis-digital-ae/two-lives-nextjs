What Happens When Your Life Changes Faster Than You Expected

2026-05-14 • By Basim Yafai

The Pressure of Becoming: What High Performers Often Carry in Silence

 

When Life Changes Faster Than You Expected

A lot of people want the next level until they actually arrive there.

The success. The visibility. The pressure. The expectation to keep proving yourself once people start watching.

From the outside, it can look exciting. But internally, it can feel very different when your life changes quickly and you’re trying to adapt while still performing at a high level.

This is something a lot of high performers carry quietly. Athletes, entrepreneurs, leaders, public figures. People often see the outcome, but they don’t always see what it takes mentally to sustain it.

Because once you reach a certain level, the pressure changes.

You’re no longer trying to get there.
You’re trying to stay there.

 

The Pressure Nobody Really Talks About

At first, pressure can feel motivating. It sharpens you. Pushes you. Makes you hungry.

But over time, if it’s constant, it starts affecting you differently.

You begin feeling:

  • Pressure to maintain your level.
  • Pressure to not fall backwards.
  • Pressure to constantly perform.
  • Pressure to carry expectations from other people.

And when that builds quietly over years, it can start affecting your wellbeing without you fully noticing at first.

Not because you’re weak. Because you’re human.

A lot of high performers become so used to surviving pressure that they stop recognising how much they’re actually carrying internally.

 

Footballer 'Jamie Vardy' is a Powerful Example of This

Jamie Vardy went from non-league football to becoming one of the most recognised players in the Premier League within five years.

That kind of rise changes everything.

One minute, you’re chasing an opportunity that most people don’t believe will happen. The next, you’re playing in front of millions, expected to perform every single week while adapting to a completely different life personally and professionally.

People see the success story. And rightly so.

But what often gets overlooked is the mental adjustment that comes with that level of change.

The expectation.
The visibility.
The pressure to stay consistent once the world suddenly expects more from you.

A lot of athletes experience this internally, even if they never speak about it publicly.

 

Why Wellbeing Matters More Than Ever at the Top

The higher you go, the more important your internal world becomes.

Because eventually, talent and hard work are not enough on their own.

You also need:

  • Emotional control.
  • Mental clarity.
  • Boundaries.
  • Recovery.
  • Stability under pressure.

Without that, success can start feeling heavier than it should.

You can still perform externally while internally feeling anxious, disconnected, mentally drained, or constantly “on”.

This is why wellbeing should never be treated as weakness in high performance environments.

It’s part of sustaining the level you worked so hard to reach.

 

The Two Lives Theory

One of the biggest reasons people struggle during periods of rapid growth is because life changes externally faster than they adapt internally.

The Two Lives Theory is built around understanding that we are often moving between two versions of ourselves.

The life we knew, and the life we are stepping into.

A lot of people rise into a new level externally while mentally and emotionally still operating from the version of themselves that existed before everything changed.

That creates tension internally.

Part of you is trying to move forward, while another part is still trying to catch up with the speed of your new reality.

And if that tension isn’t understood properly, pressure starts building underneath the surface.

 

Supporting Yourself Through the Pressure

A lot of high performers think they need more discipline.

Most of the time, they actually need more awareness.

Awareness of:

  • What they’re carrying mentally.
  • How pressure is affecting them.
  • Where they’ve disconnected from themselves.
  • What their nervous system has been holding for years.

Because constantly surviving pressure is very different from feeling internally stable within it.

And when you begin understanding yourself properly underneath the performance, everything changes.

You still strive.
You still compete.
You still want more from life.

But you no longer feel like you’re constantly carrying the weight of success alone.

 

Final Thoughts

If this resonates with you, it’s important to understand that struggling with pressure does not mean you are failing.

In many cases, it simply means your external life has evolved faster than you’ve emotionally processed internally.

That is far more common than people realise.
