The Identity That Got You Here Won’t Get You There

2026-06-05 • By Basim Yafai

The Identity That Got You Here Won’t Get You There

 

Success Changes More Than Your Circumstances

Most people think growth is about adding something.

A better strategy, more discipline, more experience and more knowledge.

In the early stages of life, that's often true. Whether you're building a business, competing in sport, leading a company, or performing at the highest level in poker, progress usually comes from learning, improving, and finding ways to push yourself further.

But there comes a point where that approach stops creating the breakthrough you're looking for.

Not because you've stopped growing.

Because the challenge has changed.

I've worked with enough entrepreneurs, athletes, leaders, and high performers to notice a pattern. The people who feel most stuck are rarely lacking capability. They're often intelligent, driven, successful, and fully committed to what they're doing.

Yet underneath all of that, there's usually a quiet feeling that something isn't moving the way it used to.

Success changes your circumstances far quicker than it changes your identity.

Yes... This is where things start to get interesting.

 

The Hidden Pressure of Staying the Same

A lot of high performers spend years becoming a certain type of person.

  1. The entrepreneur learns to carry responsibility.
  2. The athlete learns to push through discomfort.
  3. The leader learns to hold everything together.
  4. The poker player learns to control emotion under pressure.

Those qualities matter. In many ways, they're the reason success happens in the first place.

But what people rarely talk about is how easy it is to become attached to those traits.

What starts as a strength can slowly become part of your identity.

I've seen entrepreneurs struggle to trust others because control became part of who they are. I've seen athletes continue driving themselves through self-criticism long after it stopped serving them. I've seen leaders who no longer know how to switch off because they've spent years believing their value comes from carrying everyone else.

None of these patterns are wrong. They simply belong to a version of you that was built for a different season of life.

 

Why So Many High Performers Feel Stuck

This is often where confusion begins.

From the outside, life can look successful.

  1. The business is growing.
  2. The career is progressing.
  3. The results are still there.

Yet internally, something feels heavier than it should.

Many people respond by trying to do more. More work. More effort. More pressure.

But sometimes the issue isn't that you're not doing enough.

Sometimes you're carrying an identity that no longer fits the life you've built.

Think about someone who spent ten years proving themselves. Every decision, every sacrifice, every goal was driven by the need to become successful.

Then they finally get there.

What happens when the thing you've spent years chasing is no longer the thing that's driving you?

For many people, that's when they realise they've become trapped by the version of themselves that created the success.

 

The Two Lives Theory

This is one of the reasons I created the Two Lives Theory.

Because I believe there are moments in life where you're standing between two versions of yourself.

The person you've been, and the person you're becoming.

Most people think the difficult part is moving forwards.

I don't... I think the difficult part is letting go.

Letting go of old beliefs. Old survival patterns. Old ways of thinking. Old identities that once served a purpose but are now limiting growth.

That's why transition can feel uncomfortable. Part of you wants to move towards what's next, while another part is still holding onto what feels familiar.

The tension isn't a sign that something is wrong. It's often a sign that growth is already happening.

 

What This Can Look Like

You might recognise it as:

  1. Feeling disconnected despite achieving more.
  2. Constantly chasing the next goal without enjoying the current one.
  3. Feeling pressure that never seems to leave.
  4. Struggling to switch off or be present.
  5. Questioning why success doesn't feel the way you expected.

For a long time, many people assume these feelings mean they need another breakthrough.

In reality, they may simply be outgrowing an old identity.

Growth isn't always about becoming more. Sometimes it's about releasing what you've outgrown.

 

The Next Chapter

The most successful people I've worked with eventually realise something important.

The next level of life isn't always achieved through force.

It often comes through awareness.

Through understanding what you're carrying.

Through recognising the beliefs, habits, and identities that helped you survive one chapter but may be preventing you from fully stepping into the next.

That doesn't mean abandoning who you've been.

It means appreciating that version of yourself while creating space for something new to emerge... And that's a very different type of growth.

 

My Final Thoughts

The identity that got you here deserves respect.

It helped you overcome challenges, build resilience, and achieve things that once felt impossible.

But there comes a point where growth asks a different question.

Not "What do I need to do next?"

But:

"Who do I need to stop being in order to become who I'm capable of becoming?"

If you've been feeling stuck, disconnected, or caught between two chapters of your life, it may not be a sign that something is wrong.

It may simply be a sign that you've outgrown who you've been, and that is often where the most meaningful growth begins.
